# Research protocol

## Source priority

1. Official election notices and election authority records for dates, rules, candidates, and voting logistics.
2. Candidate-owned sites, pages, programmes, interviews, and videos for attributed candidate positions.
3. News and community discussions for leads and questions, never as sole proof of a disputed fact.

## Facebook scope

The research browser may use Peter's signed-in session to **read** accessible pages, groups, posts, and videos. It must never post, comment, react, share, follow, message, change account settings, or publish in Peter's name. It must not inspect credentials or session tokens. No unattended bulk harvesting or completeness claim based on Facebook search alone.

The researcher should also discover all registered candidates' official or self-identified public sources, using the same search standard for each. A group thread indicates that the larger Bernolákovo group limits political posts; record this as a coverage constraint, not a conclusion that there is no election discussion.

## Research record

For every relevant item, record: source ID and canonical URL; author/publisher; original and observed dates; medium (post, video, site, notice); short neutral summary; direct quotes only when needed; candidate/topic tags; verification status and corroborating URL; whether it is suitable for public use; and any access or date ambiguity. Keep raw notes private. Do not commit copied personal comments, full transcripts, or private-group material.

## Coverage ledger

Report per source: date range attempted, search terms and browsing method, dates actually reached, number of relevant items reviewed, inaccessible items or videos, and remaining gaps. Label a pass **partial** unless the evidence supports complete coverage. Public-source research should include the official candidate list, voting logistics, and available candidate programmes/interviews.
